# Mansfield Park
A young girl named [[Fanny Price]] comes to live with her wealthy uncle and aunt, [[Sir Thomas]] and [[Lady Bertram]]. [[Fanny Price|Fanny]]'s family is quite poor; her mother, unlike her sister [[Lady Bertram]], married beneath her, and [[Fanny Price|Fanny]]'s father, a sailor, is disabled and drinks heavily. [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] is abused by her other aunt, [[Mrs. Norris]], a busybody who runs things at Mansfield Park, the Bertrams' estate. The Bertram daughters, [[Maria Bertram|Maria]] and [[Julia Bertram|Julia]], are shallow, rather cruel girls, intent on marrying well and being fashionable. The elder son, [[Tom Bertram|Tom]], is a roustabout and a drunk. [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] finds solace only in the friendship of the younger son, [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]], who is planning to be a clergyman. [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] grows up shy and deferential, caught as she typically is between members of the Bertram family.

[[Sir Thomas]] leaves Mansfield Park for Antigua, where he owns plantations. In his absence, two new figures arrive at Mansfield: [[Henry Crawford|Henry]] and [[Mary Crawford]], the brother and sister of the local minister's wife. [[Henry Crawford|Henry]] and [[Mary Crawford|Mary]] are attractive and cheerful, and they soon become indispensable members of the Mansfield circle. [[Henry Crawford|Henry]] flirts extensively with [[Maria Bertram|Maria]], who is engaged to marry the boring but wealthy [[James Rushworth]]. He also flirts with [[Julia Bertram|Julia]] when it suits his purposes. At first, [[Mary Crawford|Mary]] is interested in [[Tom Bertram|Tom]], the older son and heir, but she soon realizes that he is boring and not really interested in her. She finds herself increasingly attracted to [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]], although the prospect of marrying a clergyman does not appeal to her, and she is often cruel to him on this account. In the meantime, [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] has innocently fallen in love with [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]], although she does not even admit this to herself. [[John Yates]], a visiting friend of [[Tom Bertram|Tom]]'s, proposes that the group should put on a play. His idea is eagerly received by all except for [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]] and [[Fanny Price|Fanny]], who are horrified at the idea of acting. The play goes on anyways, however; [[Maria Bertram|Maria]] and [[Henry Crawford|Henry]], as well as [[Mary Crawford|Mary]] and [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]] (who has been prevailed upon to take a role to avoid bringing in an outsider to play it), get to play some rather racy scenes with one another. When one of the women cannot make a rehearsal, [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] is pressured to take a role. She is almost forced to give in when [[Sir Thomas]] makes a sudden entrance, having arrived from Antigua.

[[Sir Thomas]] is unhappy about the play and quickly puts a stop to the improprieties. Since [[Henry Crawford|Henry]] has not declared his love, [[Maria Bertram|Maria]] is married to [[James Rushworth]]. [[Maria Bertram|She]] and [[Julia Bertram|Julia]] leave Mansfield Park for London. Relationships between the Crawfords and the Bertrams intensify. [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]] nearly proposes to [[Mary Crawford|Mary]] several times, but her condescension and amorality always stop him at the last minute. He confides his feelings to [[Fanny Price|Fanny]], who is secretly upset by them. In the meantime, on a lark, [[Henry Crawford|Henry]] has decided to woo [[Fanny Price|Fanny]]. He is surprised to find himself sincerely in love with her. [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] has become indispensable as a companion to her aunt and uncle, and on the occasion of her brother [[William Price|William]]'s visit, they give a ball in her honor. Some time after the ball, [[Henry Crawford|Henry]] helps [[William Price|William]] get a promotion in the Navy. Using this as leverage, he proposes to [[Fanny Price|Fanny]], who is mortified and refuses. He continues to pursue her. [[Sir Thomas|Her uncle]] is disappointed that she has refused such a wealthy man, and, as an indirect result, she is sent to stay with her parents in their filthy house. Meanwhile, [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]] has been ordained and continues to debate over his relationship with [[Mary Crawford|Mary]], to [[Fanny Price|Fanny]]'s dismay.

[[Henry Crawford|Henry]] comes to see [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] at her parents' and renews his suit. He then leaves to take care of business on his estate. [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] continues to receive letters from [[Mary Crawford|Mary]] encouraging her to take [[Henry Crawford|Henry]]'s proposal. A series of events then happen in rapid succession: [[Tom Bertram]] falls dangerously ill as a result of his partying and nearly dies; [[Henry Crawford|Henry]], who has gone not to his estate but to see friends, has run off with the married [[Maria Bertram|Maria]]; [[Julia Bertram|Julia]], upset over her sister's rash act, elopes with [[John Yates]], [[Tom Bertram|Tom]]'s friend. [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] is recalled to Mansfield, bringing her younger sister [[Susan Price|Susan]] with her. [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]] has finally seen through [[Mary Crawford|Mary]], who has admitted that she would like to see [[Tom Bertram|Tom]] die so that [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]] could be heir, and who has more or less condoned [[Henry Crawford|Henry]] and [[Maria Bertram|Maria]]'s actions. He is heartbroken, but [[Fanny Price|Fanny]] consoles him. [[Maria Bertram|Maria]] and [[Henry Crawford|Henry]] eventually split, and she goes to the Continent to live with the evil [[Mrs. Norris]]. [[Julia Bertram|Julia]] and [[John Yates]] are reconciled to the family. [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]] finally comes to his senses and marries [[Fanny Price|Fanny]], and [[Susan Price|Susan]] takes her place with the Bertrams. [[Edmund Bertram|Edmund]], [[Fanny Price|Fanny]], and the rest of those at Mansfield live happily, while [[Henry Crawford|Henry]], [[Mary Crawford|Mary]], and [[Maria Bertram|Maria]] are cast out.
