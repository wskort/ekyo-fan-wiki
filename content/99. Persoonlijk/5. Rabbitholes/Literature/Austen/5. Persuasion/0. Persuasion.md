# Persuasion
_Persuasion_ opens with a brief history of the Elliot family as recorded in [[Sir Walter Elliot]]'s favorite book, _The Baronetcy._ We learn that the Elliots are a respected, titled, landowning family. [[Lady Elliot]], [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]]'s wife died fourteen years ago and left him with three daughters: [[Elizabeth Elliot|Elizabeth]], [[Anne Elliot|Anne]], and [[Mary Musgrove|Mary]]. Both [[Elizabeth Elliot|Elizabeth]] and [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] are single, but [[Mary Musgrove|Mary]], the youngest is married to a wealthy man named [[Charles Musgrove]]; they live close by. [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]], who lavishly overspends, has brought the family into great debt. When [[Lady Russell]], a trusted family advisor, suggests that the Elliots reduce their spending, [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]] is horrified. He is exceedingly vain and cannot bear to imagine life without his usual comforts. But with no other option, the Elliots decide they must relocate to a house in Bath where their expenses will be more manageable. They intend to rent the family estate, Kellynch Hall.

They soon find excellent tenants to rent their home; [[Admiral Croft|Admiral]] and [[Mrs. Croft]] are wealthy and well-mannered Navy people who have a model marriage. [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]] is relieved that the [[Admiral Croft|Admiral]] is a good-looking man. Though [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]] dislikes that the Navy brings "men of obscure birth into undue distinction," he is satisfied with [[Admiral Croft|Admiral]] and [[Mrs. Croft]] as tenants for his home. [[Anne Elliot]], the middle daughter, is also excited to meet the [[Admiral Croft|Cro]][[Mrs. Croft|fts]]; [[Mrs. Croft]] is the sister of the man [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] loves. Eight years ago, she was engaged to be married to [[Captain Frederick Wentworth]], but [[Lady Russell]] persuaded her that [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]] was not of high enough consequence, and [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] called off the engagement. With the [[Admiral Croft|Cro]][[Mrs. Croft|fts]] at Kellynch, [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] hopes to see [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]] again.

[[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]], [[Elizabeth Elliot|Elizabeth]], and [[Penelope Clay]] (a widowed, somewhat lower-class friend of the family) leave for Bath. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] goes to stay with her sister [[Mary Musgrove|Mary]] at Uppercross Cottage for a period of two months. [[Mary Musgrove|Mary]] complains often and [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] patiently listens to her sister's worries. At Uppercross, [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] finds the Musgrove family delightful. [[Mr. Musgrove|Mr.]] and [[Mrs. Musgrove]] have three grown children: [[Charles Musgrove|Charles]] ([[Mary Musgrove|Mary]]'s husband), [[Henrietta Musgrove|Henrietta]], and [[Louisa Musgrove|Louisa]]. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] marvels at the bustling nature of the household and the Musgroves' clear affection for their children. Soon news comes that [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]] has returned from sea and is staying with [[Mrs. Croft|his sister]] at Kellynch. [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]] makes friends with [[Mr. Musgrove]], and he becomes a daily visitor at Uppercross. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] is at first anxious to see him again after such a long time, but his actions toward her are merely detached and polite. He seems more smitten with [[Henrietta Musgrove|Henrietta]] and [[Louisa Musgrove]]. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] resigns herself to the idea that she has lost [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]]'s love forever.

[[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]] proposes that they all take a trip to Lyme to go visit his friends the [[Captain Harville|Harvilles]]. While they are there, a good-looking gentleman takes notice of [[Anne Elliot|Anne]]; they later discover that this man is [[William Elliot]], [[Anne Elliot|Anne]]'s cousin and [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]]' s heir to Kellynch. The group decides to go for a morning walk on the beach. [[Louisa Musgrove]] has a bad fall and is knocked unconscious. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] keeps a level head and does all she can to care for [[Louisa Musgrove|Louisa]]. The doctor determines that [[Louisa Musgrove|Louisa]] will recover, but she will have to remain in Lyme for several months. [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]] blames himself for [[Louisa Musgrove|Louisa]]'s fall and tries to help the Musgrove family. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] returns to Uppercross to help [[Mr. Musgrove|Mr.]] and [[Mrs. Musgrove]] care for their younger children. After a few weeks, she leaves to stay with [[Lady Russell]].

After Christmas, [[Lady Russell]] and [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] decide that they must rejoin the rest of the Elliot family in Bath, much to [[Anne Elliot|Anne]]'s dismay. [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]] and [[Elizabeth Elliot|Elizabeth]] care little about her, but they are glad to have her come to Bath. In Bath, she is formally introduced to her cousin [[William Elliot]], who has made peace with his once estranged uncle, [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]]. Though she questions [[William Elliot]]'s motives for his sudden apology, she accepts him as a pleasing gentleman. [[William Elliot]] is extraordinarily appreciative of [[Anne Elliot|Anne]], and it is soon apparent that he seeks to make her his wife. While in Bath, [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] becomes reacquainted with an old school friend, [[Mrs. Smith]], who has recently been widowed and fallen into hard times. From [[Mrs. Smith]], [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] learns about [[William Elliot]]'s hidden past; she finds out that he has mistreated [[Mrs. Smith]] and that he plans to marry [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] to ensure that he becomes the sole heir of the Kellynch baronetcy. [[William Elliot]] fears that [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]] will marry [[Penelope Clay]], have a son, and thereby deprive him of his title. He plots to ensure that he will remain [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]]'s heir. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] is appalled to hear this news.

The [[Admiral Croft|Cro]][[Mrs. Croft|fts]] arrive in Bath with news of two engagements; [[Henrietta Musgrove|Henrietta]] will marry her cousin [[Charles Hayter]], and [[Louisa Musgrove|Louisa]] will marry [[Captain Benwick]], a man she met at Lyme while she was convalescing. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] is overjoyed that [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]] is not promised to [[Louisa Musgrove|Louisa]] and is free once again. [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]] soon arrives in Bath. He is now a much richer man than he was eight years ago, and [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]] reluctantly admits him into their social circle. [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Wentworth]] grows jealous because he believes [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] is attached to her cousin [[William Elliot]]. Yet he writes [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] a love letter in which he describes his true, constant, and undying love for her. [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] is thrilled and they become engaged. [[William Elliot]] is shocked that his plan to marry [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] has been foiled. He and [[Penelope Clay]] leave Bath. It is rumored that they are together. There is no longer any danger that [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]] will marry beneath his station. [[Sir Walter Elliot|Sir Walter]] and [[Lady Russell]] give their approval for the marriage between [[Anne Elliot|Anne]] and [[Captain Frederick Wentworth|Captain Wentworth]].
