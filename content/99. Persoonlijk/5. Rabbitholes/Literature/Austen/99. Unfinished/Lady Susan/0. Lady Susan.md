# Lady Susan
In a letter, the poor, recently widowed [[Lady Susan Vernon]] invites herself to stay at Churchill, the estate of her brother-in-law, [[Charles Vernon]], and her sister-in-law, [[Catherine Vernon]]. She reveals in a different letter to her friend, [[Alicia Johnson]], that this isn’t by choice—she was previously staying with a married man, [[Mr. Manwaring]], and his wife, [[Mrs. Manwaring]]. While she was there, she seduced [[Mr. Manwaring]] and a man named [[Sir James Martin]], intending to secure [[Sir James Martin|Sir James]] as a husband for her daughter, [[Frederica Vernon|Frederica]]. However, [[Sir James Martin|Sir James]] was already engaged to [[Mr. Manwaring]]’s sister, [[Miss Manwaring]]. In the wake of the chaos she caused, [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]] had to leave, and she now plans to drop [[Frederica Vernon|Frederica]] off at boarding school en route to Churchill. Meanwhile, corresponding with her mother [[Lady De Courcy]] and brother [[Reginald De Courcy]], [[Catherine Vernon|Catherine]]  notes that [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]] has a bad reputation—only [[Reginald De Courcy|Reginald]], hoping to be amused, is excited about her visit. Six years before, [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]] tried to prevent [[Charles Vernon|Charles]] from marrying [[Catherine Vernon|Catherine]], and [[Catherine Vernon|Catherine]] hasn’t forgiven her.

When [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]] arrives, her behavior doesn’t match up with her reputation, and she’s able to charm [[Charles Vernon|Charles]]—though [[Catherine Vernon|Catherine]] recognizes that [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]] has a talent for lying. In a letter to [[Alicia Johnson|Alicia]], [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]] reveals that she plans to force [[Frederica Vernon|Frederica]] to marry [[Sir James Martin|Sir James]] against her will—boarding school is just a temporary punishment. Meanwhile, [[Reginald De Courcy|Reginald]] begins to change his opinion of [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]], prolonging his stay to spend time with her; while [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]] has no plans to marry him and is still toying with [[Mr. Manwaring|Manwaring]] from afar, she enjoys tormenting [[Catherine Vernon|Catherine]] by flirting with [[Reginald De Courcy|Reginald]]. [[Catherine Vernon|Catherine]], believing that [[Reginald De Courcy|Reginald]] might actually be in love with [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]], shares her concerns with [[Lady De Courcy]]—but her letter finds its way to her father, [[Sir Reginald De Courcy]]. [[Sir Reginald De Courcy|Sir Reginald]] writes to [[Reginald De Courcy|Reginald]], disapproving of [[Lady Susan Vernon|Lady Susan]], both because she would make [[Reginald De Courcy|Reginald]] unhappy and because her reputation would damage the family. [[Reginald De Courcy|Reginald]] responds that he won’t marry her, but that her reputation is undeserved—[[Catherine Vernon|Catherine]], however, still believes that the match will happen one day.
