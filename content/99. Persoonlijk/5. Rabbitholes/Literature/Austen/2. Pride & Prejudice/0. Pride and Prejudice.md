# Pride and Prejudice
The news that a wealthy young gentleman named [[Charles Bingley]] has rented the manor of Netherfield Park causes a great stir in the nearby village of Longbourn, especially in the Bennet household. The [[Mr. Bennet|B]][[Mrs. Bennet|e]][[Jane Bennet|n]][[Elizabeth Bennet|n]][[Mary Bennet|e]][[Catherine Bennet|t]][[Lydia Bennet|s]] have five unmarried daughters—from oldest to youngest, [[Jane Bennet|Jane]], [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]], [[Mary Crawford|Mary]], [[Catherine Bennet|Kitty]], and [[Lydia Bennet|Lydia]]—and [[Mrs. Bennet]] is desperate to see them all married. After [[Mr. Bennet]] pays a social visit to [[Charles Bingley|Mr. Bingley]], the [[Mr. Bennet|B]][[Mrs. Bennet|e]][[Jane Bennet|n]][[Elizabeth Bennet|n]][[Mary Bennet|e]][[Catherine Bennet|t]][[Lydia Bennet|s]] attend a ball at which [[Charles Bingley|Mr. Bingley]] is present. He is taken with [[Jane Bennet|Jane]] and spends much of the evening dancing with her. His close friend, [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Mr. Darcy]], is less pleased with the evening and haughtily refuses to dance with [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]], which makes everyone view him as arrogant and obnoxious.

At social functions over subsequent weeks, however, [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Mr. Darcy]] finds himself increasingly attracted to [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]]’s charm and intelligence. [[Jane Bennet|Jane]]’s friendship with [[Charles Bingley|Mr. Bingley]] also continues to burgeon, and [[Jane Bennet|Jane]] pays a visit to the Bingley mansion. On her journey to the house she is caught in a downpour and catches ill, forcing her to stay at Netherfield for several days. In order to tend to [[Jane Bennet|Jane]], [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] hikes through muddy fields and arrives with a spattered dress, much to the disdain of the snobbish [[Caroline Bingley]], [[Charles Bingley]]’s sister. [[Caroline Bingley]]’s spite only increases when she notices that [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]], whom she is pursuing, pays quite a bit of attention to [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]].

When [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] and [[Jane Bennet|Jane]] return home, they find [[Mr. Collins]] visiting their household. [[Mr. Collins]] is a young clergyman who stands to inherit [[Mr. Bennet]]’s property, which has been “entailed,” meaning that it can only be passed down to male heirs. [[Mr. Collins]] is a pompous fool, though he is quite enthralled by the Bennet girls. Shortly after his arrival, he makes a proposal of marriage to [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]]. She turns him down, wounding his pride. Meanwhile, the [[Jane Bennet|B]][[Elizabeth Bennet|e]][[Mary Bennet|nn]][[Catherine Bennet|e]][[Lydia Bennet|t]] girls have become friendly with militia officers stationed in a nearby town. Among them is [[George Wickham]], a handsome young soldier who is friendly toward [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] and tells her how [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] cruelly cheated him out of an inheritance.

At the beginning of winter, the [[Charles Bingley|Bing]][[Caroline Bingley|leys]] and [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] leave Netherfield and return to London, much to [[Jane Bennet|Jane]]’s dismay. A further shock arrives with the news that [[Mr. Collins]] has become engaged to [[Charlotte Lucas]], [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]]’s best friend and the poor daughter of a local knight. [[Charlotte Lucas|Charlotte]] explains to [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] that she is getting older and needs the match for financial reasons. [[Charlotte Lucas|Charlotte]] and [[Mr. Collins]] get married and [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] promises to visit them at their new home. As winter progresses, [[Jane Bennet|Jane]] visits the city to see friends (hoping also that she might see [[Charles Bingley|Mr. Bingley]]). However, [[Caroline Bingley]] visits her and behaves rudely, while [[Charles Bingley|Mr. Bingley]] fails to visit her at all. The marriage prospects for the Bennet girls appear bleak.

That spring, [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] visits [[Charlotte Lucas|Charlotte]], who now lives near the home of [[Mr. Collins]]’s patron, [[Lady Catherine de Bourgh]], who is also [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]]’s aunt. [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] calls on [[Lady Catherine de Bourgh|Lady Catherine]] and encounters [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]], whose presence leads him to make a number of visits to the [[Mr. Collins|Coll]][[Charlotte Lucas|ins]]’s home, where she is staying. One day, he makes a shocking proposal of marriage, which [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] quickly refuses. She tells [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] that she considers him arrogant and unpleasant, then scolds him for steering [[Charles Bingley|Bingley]] away from [[Jane Bennet|Jane]] and disinheriting [[George Wickham]]. [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] leaves her but shortly thereafter delivers a letter to her. In this letter, he admits that he urged [[Charles Bingley|Bingley]] to distance himself from [[Jane Bennet|Jane]], but claims he did so only because he thought their romance was not serious. As for [[George Wickham]], he informs [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] that the young officer is a liar and that the real cause of their disagreement was [[George Wickham]]’s attempt to elope with his young sister, [[Georgiana Darcy]].

This letter causes [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] to reevaluate her feelings about [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]]. She returns home and acts coldly toward [[George Wickham]]. The militia is leaving town, which makes the younger, rather man-crazy Bennet girls distraught. [[Lydia Bennet|Lydia]] manages to obtain permission from her father to spend the summer with an old colonel in Brighton, where [[George Wickham]]’s regiment will be stationed. With the arrival of June, [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] goes on another journey, this time with the [[Mr. Gardiner|Gardiners]], who are relatives of the [[Mr. Bennet|B]][[Mrs. Bennet|e]][[Jane Bennet|n]][[Elizabeth Bennet|n]][[Mary Bennet|e]][[Catherine Bennet|t]][[Lydia Bennet|s]]. The trip takes her to the North and eventually to the neighborhood of Pemberley, [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]]’s estate. She visits Pemberley, after making sure that [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] is away, and delights in the building and grounds, while hearing from [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]]’s servants that he is a wonderful, generous master. Suddenly, [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] arrives and behaves cordially toward her. Making no mention of his proposal, he entertains the [[Mr. Gardiner|Gardiners]] and invites [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] to meet [[Georgiana Darcy|his sister]].

Shortly thereafter, however, a letter arrives from home, telling [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] that [[Lydia Bennet|Lydia]] has eloped with [[George Wickham]] and that the couple is nowhere to be found, which suggests that they may be living together out of wedlock. Fearful of the disgrace such a situation would bring on her entire family, [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] hastens home. [[Mr. Gardiner]] and [[Mr. Bennet]] go off to search for [[Lydia Bennet|Lydia]], but [[Mr. Bennet]] eventually returns home empty-handed. Just when all hope seems lost, a letter comes from [[Mr. Gardiner]] saying that the couple has been found and that Wickham has agreed to marry [[Lydia Bennet|Lydia]] in exchange for an annual income. The [[Mr. Bennet|B]][[Mrs. Bennet|e]][[Jane Bennet|n]][[Elizabeth Bennet|n]][[Mary Bennet|e]][[Catherine Bennet|t]][[Lydia Bennet|s]] are convinced that [[Mr. Gardiner]] has paid off [[George Wickham]], but [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] learns that the source of the money, and of her family’s salvation, was none other than [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]].

Now married, [[George Wickham]] and [[Lydia Bennet|Lydia]] return to Longbourn briefly, where [[Mr. Bennet]] treats them coldly. They then depart for [[George Wickham]]’s new assignment in the North of England. Shortly thereafter, [[Charles Bingley|Bingley]] returns to Netherfield and resumes his courtship of [[Jane Bennet|Jane]]. [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] goes to stay with him and pays visits to the [[Mr. Bennet|B]][[Mrs. Bennet|e]][[Jane Bennet|n]][[Elizabeth Bennet|n]][[Mary Bennet|e]][[Catherine Bennet|ts]] but makes no mention of his desire to marry [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]]. [[Charles Bingley|Bingley]], on the other hand, presses his suit and proposes to [[Jane Bennet|Jane]], to the delight of everyone but [[Charles Bingley|Bingley]]’s [[Caroline Bingley|haughty sister]]. While the family celebrates, [[Lady Catherine de Bourgh]] pays a visit to Longbourn. She corners [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] and says that she has heard that [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]], her nephew, is planning to marry her. Since she considers a Bennet an unsuitable match for a [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]], [[Lady Catherine de Bourgh|Lady Catherine]] demands that [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] promise to refuse him. [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] spiritedly refuses, saying she is not engaged to [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]], but she will not promise anything against her own happiness. A little later, [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] and [[Fitzwilliam Darcy|Darcy]] go out walking together and he tells her that his feelings have not altered since the spring. She tenderly accepts his proposal, and both [[Jane Bennet|Jane]] and [[Elizabeth Bennet|Elizabeth]] are married.
