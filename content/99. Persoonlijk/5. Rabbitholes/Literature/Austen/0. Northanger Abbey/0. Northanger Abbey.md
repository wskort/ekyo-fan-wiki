# Northangar Abbey
_Northanger Abbey_ is the coming-of-age story of a young woman named [[Catherine Morland]]. It is divided into two sections, Book I and Book II. The two Books differ significantly from each other in setting and, to a degree, in tone.

Book I begins when the [[Mrs. Allen|Allens]], family friends of the Morlands, offer to take [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] with them to Bath, a resort for the wealthier members of British society. The 17-year-old [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] eagerly accepts the [[Mrs. Allen|Allens]]' invitation. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] is young and naïve. Her life has been relatively sheltered, so Bath is a new world for her. In Bath, [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] is introduced to [[Henry Tilney]], a young clergyman who impresses [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] with his wit and pleasant conversation. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] quickly falls for [[Henry Tilney|Henry]], but after their first meeting she does not see him again for some time. [[Mrs. Allen]] runs into an old acquaintance, [[Mrs. Thorpe]], and her three young daughters, including [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]], who is slightly older than [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]]. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] and [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] are soon best friends. [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]], superficial and fond of gossip, inducts [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] into the social world of Bath, with all its balls, dances, shows, fashion, and its gossip.

Just when [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] and Isabella have settled into a close friendship, they are met with the arrival of [[James Morland]], [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]]'s brother, and [[John Thorpe]], [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]]'s brother. [[James Morland|James]] and [[John Thorpe|John]] are friends at Oxford University. [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] wastes no time in flirting with [[James Morland|James]], and soon it is obvious to everyone except [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] that [[James Morland|James]] and [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] are in love. Taking a cue from [[James Morland|James]], [[John Thorpe|John]] tries to woo [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]], asking her to be his dance partner. But at a ball, [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] sees [[Henry Tilney]] again and is more interested in [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] than in [[John Thorpe|John]]. [[John Thorpe|John]]'s bragging and his arrogant nature put off [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]].

Soon all of [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]]'s time is taken up with [[James Morland|James]]. Without [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] to spend her time with and saddled with the unpleasant [[John Thorpe]], [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] decides to become friends with [[Eleanor Tilney]], [[Henry Tilney|Henry]]'s sister. [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] quickly sees that [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] has feelings for [[Henry Tilney|Henry]], but does not say anything. After rain seems to wash out her plans for a walk with [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] and [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]], [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] is pressured by [[James Morland|James]] and [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] into riding with [[John Thorpe|John]], much to her dismay. On the way, she spots [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] and [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] walking toward her house for the planned walk. [[John Thorpe|John]] refuses to stop, angering [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]].

[[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] apologizes to [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] and [[Henry Tilney|Henry]], and plans are made for another walk. [[John Thorpe|John]], [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]], and [[James Morland|James]] again intervene, pressuring [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] into another outing. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] firmly refuses this time and joins [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] and [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] in a walk around Beechen Cliff. They discuss novels, and [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] is delighted to find that [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] and [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] love books as much as she does. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] returns home to discover that [[James Morland|James]] and [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] have become engaged. She briefly meets with [[John Thorpe|John]], who is leaving Bath for several weeks. [[John Thorpe|John]] leaves with the false impression that [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] is in love with him, although [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] does not realize this.

Book II begins with the arrival of [[Henry Tilney|Henry]]'s older brother, [[Captain Frederick Tilney]]. [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] quickly catches the eye of the captain and, dismayed by the discovery of [[James Morland|James]]'s modest income, begins to flirt with [[Captain Frederick Tilney|Frederick]]. [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] invites [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] to visit the Tilney home in Northanger Abbey. The invitation is seconded by [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]]'s father, [[General Tilney]]. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] eagerly accepts the invitation, delighted at the prospect of visiting a real abbey and at seeing more of [[Henry Tilney|Henry]]. Before [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] leaves, [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] tells her that [[John Thorpe|John]] is planning to propose to [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]]. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] tells [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] to write him and tell him, with her apologies, that he is mistaken. [[Captain Frederick Tilney|Frederick]] appears and flirts with [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]], who returns his attentions. Dismayed by this behavior, [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] asks [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] to convince [[Captain Frederick Tilney|Frederick]] to leave [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] alone. [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] refuses, knowing that [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] is at least as guilty as the [[Captain Frederick Tilney|captain]], but he tells [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] that [[Captain Frederick Tilney|Frederick]] will probably leave Bath with his regiment soon anyway.

[[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] leaves with the Tilneys for Northanger Abbey. On the way, [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] tells [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] how she imagines the Abbey to resemble the haunted ruins of the Gothic novels she loves. [[Henry Tilney|Henry]], amused, responds by giving a hypothetical account of her first night at the Abbey, complete with mysterious chests, violent storms, and secret passages. Northanger Abbey turns out to be quite dull, having been fixed up by [[General Tilney]]. Due to her overactive imagination, [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] entertains all sorts of frightening ideas about the place, each of which is thwarted. For instance, a strange bureau in [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]]'s room turns out to contain nothing more mysterious than receipts. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] becomes intrigued by the death of [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] and [[Henry Tilney|Henry]]'s mother years earlier. Her mind full of Gothic plots, [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] suspects [[General Tilney]] of murdering his wife. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] sneaks into the mother's old chamber and discovers nothing. She is caught by [[Henry Tilney|Henry]], who guesses her thoughts and scolds her. Mortified and ashamed, [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] quickly resumes her good behavior.

[[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] receives a letter from [[James Morland|her brother]] telling her that his engagement to [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] has been called off. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] thinks that [[Captain Frederick Tilney|Frederick]] forced himself between them, but [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] convinces her that it was as much [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]]'s fault as [[Captain Frederick Tilney|Frederick]]'s. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] visits [[Henry Tilney|Henry]]'s house at Woodston. The [[General Tilney|General]] drops hints about [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] marrying [[Henry Crawford|Henry]]. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] gets another letter, this time from [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]], telling her that [[Captain Frederick Tilney|Frederick]] has left her, and asking [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] to apologize to [[James Morland|James]] for her. Angry at being manipulated, [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] wishes she had never known [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]]. The [[General Tilney|General]] leaves on a business trip, and [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] goes back to Woodston for several days. The General then returns unexpectedly and tells [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] to send [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] away the next morning. Though she is very embarrassed, [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] has no choice but to send [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] to her home in Fullerton.

[[Catherine Morland|Catherine]]'s family is irritated by the [[General Tilney|General]]'s rudeness, but is glad to have her home. [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] mopes around, despondent, until suddenly [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] arrives in Fullerton and proposes to her. [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] explains that his father's behavior was due to [[John Thorpe]]. In Bath, when [[John Thorpe|John]] thought [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] loved him, he had told [[General Tilney]] that [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] was from a very wealthy family. When the General ran into [[John Thorpe|John]] much later, after [[Isabella Thorpe|Isabella]] had told [[John Thorpe|John]] about [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]]'s true feelings, [[John Thorpe|John]] had angrily told the [[General Tilney|General]] that the Morlands were almost poor. Mortified, the General had sent [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] away, furious that his hopes for [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] to make a wealthy match were to be frustrated. [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] and [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]] decide to wait until the [[General Tilney|General]] gives his consent to their marriage. Within a few months, [[Eleanor Tilney|Eleanor]] marries a very wealthy and important man, which puts the [[General Tilney|General]] in a good mood. Once he is told of the true nature of the Morland's financial situation, which is moderate, he gives his consent, and the novel ends with the marriage of [[Henry Tilney|Henry]] and [[Catherine Morland|Catherine]].
