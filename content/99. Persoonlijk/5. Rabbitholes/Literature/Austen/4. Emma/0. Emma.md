# Emma
Although convinced that she herself will never marry, [[Emma Woodhouse]], a precocious twenty-year-old resident of the village of Highbury, imagines herself to be naturally gifted in conjuring love matches. After self-declared success at matchmaking between [[Anne Weston|her governess]] and [[Mr. Weston]], a village widower, [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] takes it upon herself to find an eligible match for her new friend, [[Harriet Smith]]. Though [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]]’s parentage is unknown, [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] is convinced that Harriet deserves to be a gentleman’s wife and sets her friend’s sights on [[Mr. Elton]], the village vicar. Meanwhile, [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] persuades [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] to reject the proposal of [[Robert Martin]], a well-to-do farmer for whom [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] clearly has feelings.

[[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] becomes infatuated with [[Mr. Elton]] under [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]]’s encouragement, but [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]]’s plans go awry when [[Mr. Elton|Elton]] makes it clear that his affection is for [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]], not [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]]. [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] realizes that her obsession with making a match for [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] has blinded her to the true nature of the situation. [[George Knightley]], [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]]’s brother-in-law and treasured friend, watches [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]]’s matchmaking efforts with a critical eye. He believes that [[Robert Martin|Mr. Martin]] is a worthy young man whom [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] would be lucky to marry. He and [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] quarrel over [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]]’s meddling, and, as usual, [[George Knightley]] proves to be the wiser of the pair. [[Mr. Elton|Elton]], spurned by [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] and offended by her insinuation that [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] is his equal, leaves for the town of Bath and marries a girl there almost immediately.

[[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] is left to comfort [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] and to wonder about the character of a new visitor expected in Highbury—[[Mr. Weston]]’s son, [[Frank Churchill]]. [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] is set to visit his father in Highbury after having been raised by his aunt and uncle in London, who have taken him as their heir. [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] knows nothing about [[Frank Churchill|Frank]], who has long been deterred from visiting his father by his aunt’s illnesses and complaints. [[George Knightley]] is immediately suspicious of the young man, especially after [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] rushes back to London merely to have his hair cut. [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]], however, finds [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] delightful and notices that his charms are directed mainly toward her. Though she plans to discourage these charms, she finds herself flattered and engaged in a flirtation with the young man. [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] greets [[Jane Fairfax]], another addition to the Highbury set, with less enthusiasm. [[Jane Fairfax|Jane]] is beautiful and accomplished, but [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] dislikes her because of her reserve and, the narrator insinuates, because she is jealous of [[Jane Fairfax|Jane]].

Suspicion, intrigue, and misunderstandings ensue. [[George Knightley]] defends [[Jane Fairfax|Jane]], saying that she deserves compassion because, unlike [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]], she has no independent fortune and must soon leave home to work as a governess. [[Anne Weston]] suspects that the warmth of [[George Knightley]]’s defense comes from romantic feelings, an implication [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] resists. Everyone assumes that [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] and [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] are forming an attachment, though [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] soon dismisses [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] as a potential suitor and imagines him as a match for [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]]. At a village ball, [[George Knightley|Knightley]] earns [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]]’s approval by offering to dance with [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]], who has just been humiliated by [[Mr. Elton]] and his new wife. The next day, [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] saves [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] from Romani beggars. When [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] tells [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] that she has fallen in love with a man above her social station, [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] believes that she means [[Frank Churchill|Frank]]. [[George Knightley|Knightley]] begins to suspect that [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] and [[Jane Fairfax|Jane]] have a secret understanding, and he attempts to warn [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]]. [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] laughs at [[George Knightley|Knightley]]’s suggestion and loses [[George Knightley|Knightley]]’s approval when she flirts with [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] and insults [[Miss Bates]], a kindhearted spinster and [[Jane Fairfax|Jane]]’s aunt, at a picnic. When [[George Knightley|Knightley]] reprimands [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]], she weeps.

News comes that [[Frank Churchill|Frank]]’s aunt has died, and this event paves the way for an unexpected revelation that slowly solves the mysteries. [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] and [[Jane Fairfax|Jane]] have been secretly engaged; his attentions to [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] have been a screen to hide his true preference. With his aunt’s death and his uncle’s approval, [[Frank Churchill|Frank]] can now marry [[Jane Fairfax|Jane]], the woman he loves. [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] worries that [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] will be crushed, but she soon discovers that it is [[George Knightley|Knightley]], not [[Frank Churchill|Frank]], who is the object of [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]]’s affection. [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] believes that [[George Knightley|Knightley]] shares her feelings. [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] finds herself upset by [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]]’s revelation, and her distress forces her to realize that she is in love with [[George Knightley|Knightley]]. [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] expects [[George Knightley|Knightley]] to tell her he loves [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]], but, to her delight, [[George Knightley|Knightley]] declares his love for [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]]. [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] is soon comforted by a second proposal from [[Robert Martin]], which she accepts. The novel ends with the marriage of [[Harriet Smith|Harriet]] and [[Robert Martin|Mr. Martin]] and that of [[Emma Woodhouse|Emma]] and [[George Knightley]], resolving the question of who loves whom after all.
