# Yin and Yang

Yin and Yang. Two parts of a whole.
One stands for the positive, the light, the day, the white, the uplifting, and strangely enough, the masculine.
As if men are a <em>good</em> thing.
And on the other hand, there is the negative, the dark, the night, the black, the down falling, and altogether feminine.
The idiot who came up with this must have been a man raised by dogs or something. Clearly, the notion that women actually give <em>life</em> escaped him.
He was probably never breastfed, either.
And I doubt he had siblings.
Or a mother.
Any religious man would describe his mother as an angel, after all. Jesus even considered her a straight out miracle.
My name is Lynn. Yes, I'm a goth. Yes, I hate men. But don't think that makes me typical. Most goths I know love life and adore their boy- or girlfriends. I just happen to fit the stereotype.
Don't think I avoid men at all cost, either. I'll just never, <em>ever</em> get 'hooked up'.
I have a different way of avoiding them.

Most of the time, my eyes are a blue greenish blur. I do have prescription glasses, but I never wear them. Instead, I have contacts.
However, every now and then, when I really hate the world, my eyes are a light shade of brown. Of course, this never happens when I have a test scheduled, since I can hardly see a thing without <em>proper</em> contacts.
Once upon a time, I had dark blonde hair with streaks of light blonde. Now, my hair is pitch black, with the occasional strand of purple.
As for size, I have no idea how tall I am. I'm taller than most girls, I guess. But I'm shorter than most guys.
And yes, I'm a goth. I like the music, I like the clothes, I like the style, I like the people. I don't care for the spikes, though. So I don't own any spikes.
I'm not really into the fancy corsets or the pretty dresses. Sure, they're gorgeous, but I think they suggest being 'up for grabs'. I am most definitely <em>not</em> 'up for grabs'.
No, my style is mainly in baggy pant and baggy t-shirts showing the names of my favourite bands.
Oh, and I don't have any tattoos or piercings. Not even the earlobes. I never had the discipline to wear jewellery, anyway.

<span style="text-decoration: underline;">I have very, very dark eyes, accentuated by their natural habit of not opening very far.</span>

Don't go thinking you know me now. Don't even suspect you could form an idea about me. You don't know anything about me. The way I look, the way I talk… It all means nothing. I am who I am. And since I'm not you, you can't even <em>begin</em> to relate to me.
Years of experience don't help you in dealing with me. Years of study don't help you understand me. If you were to walk a mile in my shoes, you still wouldn't have a clue as to who I am. You could know everything there is to know about me and still know nothing.
I am different from everyone. Nothing you say is going to change that. I stay the same.

<span style="text-decoration: underline;">These days, nobody tries anymore. </span>

Although they'd been in the same class for the past three years, Chang and Lynn never even had a proper introduction until Chang started dating Kirsty. Lynn was Kirsty's best friend, which made her pretty much a package deal that just came with Kirsty. And now that Kirsty was also Chang's girlfriend, he was included as a free gift. Evidently, when the trio hung out together, both Lynn and Chang clung on to Kirsty. It was a regular Tuesday afternoon, and all three teenagers had stayed in the school cafeteria much longer than the janitor would have liked. Whenever Lynn and Kirsty were engulfed in a conversation that couldn't include Chang, he would caress Kirsty's beautiful crayon-red curls until she snapped and treated herself to a passionate kiss from her new-found lover. Consequently, Lynn would feel locked out and make a remark, leading Kirsty to break the kiss for a fit of giggles. Life smiled at the three teens that Tuesday afternoon. Of course, Lynn and Chang rarely spoke to one another.

Alas, as afternoon gently flows into evening, the patience of parents gradually diminishes until nothing remains. A phone rang. Three teens grabbed for their mobiles.

"Kirsty here," said Kirsty, answering her phone. Chang put his back in his pocket with a sigh. Lynn gave up emptying her bag. "No, I'm still at school. Don't worry, I'll be home in time for supper!" said Kirsty. Then Lynn remembered something, and continued to turn her bag upside down. Kirsty paled. "… Was that <em>today</em>?" she asked. Lynn finally found <em>her</em> mobile, and switched it on. "… I'm coming right over!"

Kirsty hung up, and managed to pick herself up from her half-sitting half-lying position on top of the cafeteria table. "I have to go," she needlessly explained.

"We figured," Lynn needlessly supplied.

"Grandmamma is visiting!" she uttered, as if the mere concept confused her.

"So go!" smiled Chang.

"You don't mind?" she sulked.

"Go!" said both Lynn and Chang in would-be unison. Chang flashed a smile Lynn's way to celebrate their joint thought, but she did not return it. Kirsty sighed, and planted a quick kiss on Chang's lips. Walking off, she mouthed 'Call me!' to her best friend Lynn, and formed a 'phone' with her left hand.

"Run!" Lynn added, and Kirsty instantly complied. For a while, Chang smiled fondly to where Kirsty had ran out of sight. After yet another bad break-up, he had been very lucky to have found Kirsty. Breaking up with someone always unsettled him to an extent, but his last relationship had been particularly painful. He had sensed for a while that something was not right. For example, his girlfriend Theresa had reduced their usual greeting kisses to a simple 'Hi Chang,' which could almost be called resentful. And when they <em>did</em> kiss, she would look away afterwards. Big flashing warning-signs hovered over her head whenever she did this, but Chang stupidly chose to ignore them. He really liked this girl. Hell, he might even have been in love with Theresa. But finally, after nearly a month of dating and nearly two weeks of a growing gap between the pair, Chang finally faced up to the facts and broke the news to her.

When Chang broke the news to girls, some of them tended to cry. Some of them called him foul names. Some slapped him in the face. Others called in sick the next day. Most of them glared nastily at him for weeks on end. Occasionally, they would tell <em>very</em> painful lies (or truths) about him to all that would listen. On one occasion, some rather embarrassing photo's mysteriously appeared on the school web. It was painful enough to find that his latest ex <em>had</em> still cared for him after all. Theresa <em>didn't</em> care. He had waited too long. After a particularly bad break-up, Chang considered himself very lucky to have found Kirsty. That was probably why Lynn's words surprised him so when she finally broke their peaceful silence.

"So," she drawled, "Ran out of <em>normal</em> girls, have you?" Chang stared at Lynn. Lynn stared at the wall.

"Excuse me?" said Chang in an offended manner. Lynn continued to stare at the wall.

"For you to start <em>goths</em>, the rest of the school's female population must really hate you, huh?" she said, as calm as she could muster.

"<em>What</em>?" uttered Chang. Finally she turned to face him. His heart skipped a beat when he registered the unadulterated hate in her eyes.

"Girls <em>do</em> tend to hold a grudge after you break their hearts, you know." she spat.

Their hours of joint laziness had dulled his mind, and Chang struggled for comprehension. Lynn turned her attention back to the opposing wall. He would have expected this sort of hostility from an ex-girlfriend, but Lynn was no ex of his. Surely, this strange girl had no reason whatsoever to hate him?

Finally, Lynn felt certain that she could control her rage enough to speak again. "If you know what's best for you," she stoically remarked, "You'll stay well clear of her. She has done <em>nothing</em> to deserve <em>you</em>."

That stung Chang. Kirsty was a <em>great</em> girl, and she deserved <em>everything</em> that was bestowed upon her! "Kirsty deserves the <em>best</em>!" he thus pointed out.

"<em>Exactly</em>." sneered Lynn. Slowly Chang realised the insult was not directed to his girlfriend. Actually, it was rather offensive.

There was a beeping sound. Lynn blinked as her eyes refused to show her anything else than the wall, wherever she looked.

"That's me," she commented, as if Chang would have thought anything else. Picking up her cell-phone, her suspicions were confirmed as the display bluntly stated: 'Home is calling'. She pressed 'no'. Her rage turned into satisfaction as she noted Chang's protests appeared extinct. "Glad we agree," she said, and simply stalked off home.

There was a lingering scent of fireplaces burning cosily as the girls got deeper into the tiny park annex forest. Over an hour had passed since Lynn's confrontation with Chang, in which time she had eaten dinner and finished her homework as fast as possible. After that, she rang up Kirsty, who had been very grateful for the excuse to leave the house.

"Really, it's terrible talking to my grandmamma!" she complained as they strolled along the path.

"How's that?" Lynn asked curiously.

"I think she's going mental!" Kirsty fumed, "After five minutes she just forgets she ever said anything and repeats everything she said before!"

"Sounds familiar," Lynn commented, having heard her friend complain of her grandmother's Alzheimer's many times before.

"No, really! She told me this <em>incredibly</em> boring story of how in <em>her</em> time she could never dye her hair, like me, since hair-dye was <em>really</em> expensive, so she had this fake hairpiece she would pin into her knot so it looked as though she was blonde. And then we chatted about school for a while, and then she just started all over again!!"

"Yep," Lynn smiled, "Suppose it runs in the family or something…"

"What?" asked Kirsty, who had been too busy complaining to pay attention.

"Excessive repetitiveness." she chuckled.

Kirsty looked stung for a moment, but smiled soon after. "So!" she effectively changed the subject, jumping in front of her childhood friend, "What d'you think of Chang? Isn't he <em>wonderful</em>?" Since her childhood friend simply strolled along, she was forced to clumsily skip backwards.

"Dunno," Lynn said indecisively, "He seems harmless, anyway."

Kirsty frowned. Growing tired of walking backwards, she turned around and walked beside Lynn again. "<em>Harmless</em>?" she repeated, as though she'd never heard of such a word. The girls climbed a small hill. On top of it was a small park bench. They sat down on the backside of it, while their feet rested upon the part of the bench that was <em>originally</em> intended to sit on.

"Yeah," said Lynn, "I mean, after a psycho-killer, a pyromaniac, a cheater, a wifebeater, and two perverts, a player isn't such a bad catch, is it?" Waving aside the casual insult to all her previous boyfriends <em>and</em> Chang, Kirsty smiled.

"Oh, come on," she said, "They weren't all <em>that</em> bad. Besides, you forgot the 'nerd'."

"Good point," said a quasi-serious Lynn, "Okay, <em>mostly</em> harmless. By the way, what ever happened to Johnnie-boy? He was <em>really</em> harmless!"

Kirsty shrugged. "Jonathan bored me." Figured. Jonathan, or as Lynn liked to call him, Johnnie-boy, had been a nice, decent guy. And nice, decent guys happened to be the direct opposite of Kirsty's type. She'd only taken him on because he looked so adorable when he first asked her out. Again Kirsty frowned. "If Chang's a player, what does that make me? A slut?"

"Nah," Lynn consoled her, "Just someone Cupid likes to pick on."

Comforted by the lack of a playful insult, which would have been a shot in a deserted goal, Kirsty smiled meekly. "I thought Cupid was after <em>you</em>?"

Lynn shook her head. "Cupid hates <em>me</em>. Probably can't stand it that I'm immune to his arrows." It was true to an extent. Lynn had never experienced anything beyond innocent physical attractions. To even out the score, she had plenty of experience in rejecting. Apparently her reputation was now enough to scare guys off, because she hadn't needed to reject anyone for over half a year.

"Yeah, right," Kirsty half sneered half giggled, "Your time will come soon enough."

Lynn nodded. "Yeah, you're probably right. I'm bound to get my first kiss some time before thirty."

They both laughed. Finally, the pair got up from their coveted bench and resumed their walk.

"I did mean what I said about Chang, though," said Lynn, "He's a real heartbreaker."

Kirsty's features darkened again. "I do wonder whether it will last, yeah. But I'll just have to make the best of it, you know?" She mustered a hopeful smile, and Lynn meekly smiled back.

Lynn had always had a weak spot for Kirsty. Since she'd been actively dating Lynn had always kept an eye out for her. She'd served as a reliable back-up in chasing off the 'two perverts'. Kirsty had taken care of the 'cheater', the 'pyromaniac', and the 'nerd', but the 'psycho-killer' had given both girls a hard time for nearly a month. They now both had a restraining order against him. The 'wifebeater' had been personally terrorised into leaving by Lynn. She really wanted to tell Kirsty just how much she distrusted Chang, but Kirsty seemed to know already. So she just took on the 'it will be fine'-role.

They walked on in silence. When they crossed a clearing, Kirsty unexpectedly giggled. "Did you ever realise I'm still missing a 'dealer' and a 'loverboy'? Maybe I could trade in a spare pervert or something…"

Lynn gasped. "You <em>wouldn't</em>!"

Kirsty laughed at Lynn's dramatic reaction, and sighed. "You know what?" she asked rhetorically.

"What?" Lynn replied none-the-less.

"I feel like ice-cream!" she stated. They shared a look, and laughed again.

Chang thoughtfully ate his dinner. His family ate in silence; it was something his father deeply valued. When he ate at a friend's place, it always amazed him how even parents joined the happy chattering of their offspring. For these people, Chang imagined, this was one of the few moments they were all together to discuss everyday life. For his parents, however, this was the only time of the day they could escape from the restaurant's chaos. Chang helped waiter sometimes at weekends, mainly because it granted him with the privilege to go out later that evening, but he wouldn't want to do it for a living. He specifically hated that tiny little rule that said: "The customer is always right." So whenever some stupid tourist claimed that there was something <em>wrong</em> with his food, which was barely <em>ever</em> the case, Chang was obliged to agree and apologise for the inexistent mistake. Usually, he would take the food away, wait two minutes in the kitchen, and bring it back with another apology. After that, no complaints ever rose. Chang often thought people complained just for the hell of it. But the worst hypocrites of all were those who claimed to have been brought the wrong dish.

"What is <em>this</em>?" they would exclaim when Chang served everyone their food.

"Babi Pangang," he would calmly reply.

"This is Fu Yong Hai!" the customer would interject. And Chang would take the dish back to the kitchen.

"Table six thinks this is Fu Yong Hai," he would then say to his uncle Chen, the chef. And Chen would grin and say: "Let's make them some <em>real</em> Fu Yong Hai, then, shall we?" Some ten minutes later, the know-it-all would be perfectly content with what they thought to be Babi Pangang.

Contemplating this as he silently ate his dinner with his parents and his 12-year-old brother Lee, Chang understood perfectly well why they had made their silence a family tradition.

A phone rang. Lee, who sometimes feared these breaches of tradition, suspiciously eyed their father. Chang, who was often the cause of them, grinned. But father never responded to this interruption. By the time Chen's wife answered the phone, peace had returned to the small room in the private quarters. It wasn't until Chang's aunt came in to say it was for Chang, that father responded. One brusque movement of his head told him he was excused.

As quietly as he could he stood up and left the room. His aunt lingered briefly, looking longingly at the food, but then followed Chang to their counter. They were nearly finished eating, anyway, so it would be her shift to eat shortly. After all, no restaurant could afford to be out of staff completely, even if they <em>did</em> hold a late dinner. Chang picked up the phone.

"Hello?" he said timidly as he always did when he didn't know who was calling.

"<em>Hey</em>,<em> man</em>,<em> it's Pete</em>!<em> Listen</em>,<em> me and the guys are hanging out a Lucas' tonight</em>,<em> d'ya wanna come</em>?" Chang's friend Peter rapidly spoke. Chang hesitated.

"I don't know," he said, "I'd have to ask my –"

"<em>Yeah</em>,<em> I figured that Christa chick would have you covered in goo by now</em>! <em>Anyway</em>,<em> you sure</em>?<em> We rented some real blockbusters</em>,<em> like Sin City 'n stuff</em>.<em> Maybe Christa would like to come</em>,<em> too</em>?" In truth, Chang had intended to say 'parents', but it was useless to try and convince Peter of that.

"Kirsty," he corrected instead, "And I don't think –"

"<em>Nah</em>,<em> didn't think so</em>. <em>We're not bringing any chicks</em>, <em>either</em>. <em>But </em>you<em> can still come</em>, <em>right</em>? <em>You can even sleep over</em>, <em>if you like</em>. <em>First two hours off tomorrow</em>!" Chang frowned.

"Really?" he asked, wondering why no one told him before.

"<em>Well</em>, <em>no</em>, <em>but we're skipping anyway</em>. <em>But are you coming</em>, <em>or what</em>?"

"I –" Chang tried to interject, but it was no use.

"<em>Oh</em>, <em>suit yourself</em>. <em>If you </em>do<em> decide to stop by</em>, <em>it's at Lucas' place</em>, <em>and you have to bring your own bed 'n stuff</em>. <em>And bring some of those fortune cookies</em>, <em>I </em>love<em> those</em>! <em>Later</em>!" Peter hung up.

As always, Chang was somewhat taken aback by Peter's constant flood of words. It usually meant that Pete might as well just do a monologue, especially on the phone. In fact, it was often just that. Nevertheless, Pete was a great friend, in a sense that you never needed to feel lonesome. Pete would talk to anyone at any time. Preferably he talked to <em>everyone</em>, <em>all</em> the time. Consequently, he made more friends in a <em>day</em> than most people did in a <em>month</em>. In fact, that was how Chang had found Kirsty, as Pete felt sorry for his break-up with Theresa and advertised him to every girl he met.

Chang returned to dinner in silence and quickly finished the last of his food. He knew that as long as he still had food to eat, he wouldn't stand a chance when asking if he could hang out at Lucas'. His father spoke the very moment Chang swallowed the last of dinner.

"Who was that?" he asked.

"Peter," Chang replied, "He asked me to come to Lucas' house and watch movies." He added this detail on purpose; it meant that he would be safely inside.

"And what did you say?" Chang considered 'not much', it was, after all, the truth.

Instead he said: "I left the option open." Which was <em>nearly</em> true. <em>Pete</em> had left the option open; he just never objected to that.

Lee stood up, and after receiving a short nod from his mother, he left the room.

"Who's coming?" father continued his interrogation. Chang shrugged.

"Lucas. Peter. The guys." he said, reciting all he knew. Upon remembering, he added: "No girls." His mother looked at him with a spark of interest gleaming in her eyes, but said nothing.

"It is very late. It will be even <em>later</em> when you return."

"I can sleep over if I like." Chang said tentatively.

"No." prompted father, "It's a school night."

"First two hours off," he bargained.

"Do Lucas' parents know of this offer?" asked mother, speaking for the first time.

"Yeah," said Chang, who had no idea, "Well, his mum does." Presumably. There was a tense silence.

Father sighed. "Very well."

Chang whooped in joy. Mother coughed in a way that it could very well be laughter. Quickly he corrected himself and thanked his father in a more appropriate way. That is, by simply saying 'thanks'.

A few minutes later mother strolled into Chang's room as he collected whatever he would need.

"So…" she said, making Chang's head snap up. He hadn't heard her entering.

"Will I be receiving any calls from school tomorrow?" He blushed, and her knowing smile grew.

"I'll try not to oversleep…" he muttered.

"And why isn't Theresa coming?" Chang stared at her in awe.

"<em>Nothing </em>passes you, does it?"

"You should have known," she said proudly.

"We broke up."

Mother watched him intensely, and then said: "So what's <em>her</em> name?"

"Kirsty…!" he said, hardly believing that this woman was his mother. Was she psychic or something…?

Vaguely he remembered Pete's awkward way of saying goodbye.

"Peter said he wanted me to bring some…" From behind her back his mother produced a bag of… "Fortune cookies… Mum! Are you <em>psychic</em>?!"

She laughed warmly, and sat down beside her eldest son to ruffle his bleached hair. "Oh, Chang!" she sighed, "<em>All</em> mothers are!"

It took a while before any response came to Chang ringing the doorbell. Having lost his patience a few moments ago, Chang peered through the window. From what he could see, the TV was on, so <em>surely</em> there had to be <em>someone</em> home… Finally Lucas opened the door.

"If you come in, you can hear it, too," he teased. Grinning the pair heaved Chang's bags inside, where they were greeted by the huge pile provided by Pete, Michael and Najib. The guys themselves were less considerate. Though Peter's hand twitched in a way that might have meant 'hello', none of the youngsters seemed to take any notice of him. On the TV-screen Friends appeared to be having a row.

"You waited?" Chang asked Lucas, who was safely storing the fortune cookies before Pete noticed them.

"Not <em>really</em>," he answered, "Mikey's addicted." Sure enough, Michael's eyes were glued to the screen. But so were Peter's and Najib's.

"Just him?" Chang asked, extracting a smile from Lucas.

"You're the last one here," he said, "Well, naturally, Jamie's missing, but he'll show up within the hour, I suppose." Chang nodded. Jamie <em>never</em> arrived before ten thirty. Sometimes Chang wondered if that was the exact moment his parents went to sleep. Sure enough, he arrived at ten forty, and finally the first movie was turned on. Chang wasn't really paying attention, though. He was still troubled by Lynn's hateful words.

'<em>If you know what's best for you</em>, <em>you'll stay well clear of her</em>.'

Why? Why did she feel the need to threaten him? Did she really believe Chang meant trouble? Did she really believe he was the sort of guy Kirsty had to be shielded from? But, he was, wasn't he? No! No! He would <em>never</em> harm Kirsty! Kirsty was just wonderful, so pure, so innocent… Untainted… Never, not <em>ever</em>, would he taint her, would he hurt her, would he break her heart… Then again, he had broken many before, hadn't he? He sunk his head in his hands. Why? Why could he never just date someone without leaving her devastated, ruined?

When he abruptly blinked and focussed his eyes again, he immediately noticed Peter eyeing him with a strange look on his face. Quickly he regained his posture and reluctantly watched the movie. To be honest, he hadn't the slightest idea what it was about, but he tried to give it full attention anyway. As swiftly as he had fixed his posture, his doubts were locked in their cage once more. Lynn was just mad. She didn't know what she was talking about. She was probably just jealous of their love… or something like that. Hushing his doubtful feelings had always been one of Chang's specialties.

Some fifteen minutes later, Pete stood up.

"Can I make a phone call?"

"Sure," replied Lucas, and he paused the movie.

"No, that's all right. I'll just use the hallway." he said, and waved a cell-phone. Lucas shrugged and pressed 'play'. Chang tried to eavesdrop from his seat on the couch, but it didn't quite work out. Peter was anxious not to be overheard. The TV on the other hand was clearly not. After a few minutes a rather perplexed Peter stuck his head around the door.

"Hey, Luke?" he asked, trying to sound casual, "Could you pop in for a sec?" Signalling his confusion to Najib, Michael, Jamie and Chang, Lucas followed Peter into the hallway. They were back within a minute.

The last ten minutes of the film passed by uneventfully.

A few moments into the next, however, the doorbell rang.

Grinning, Lucas said: "Would you get that, Chang? Thanks," without even ripping his eyes from the screen.

Grunting something that luckily remained unheard, Chang got up to open the door. It was probably just Lucas' dad, anyway. A bit late though. Chang would have thought he had already retreated to upstairs, where the guys were not allowed. But when he opened the door, it was most definitely no middle-aged man that greeted him.

"Hi Chang," Kirsty said in a way that could almost be called resentful. Chang experienced a twinge of pain. She sounded just like Theresa had in those two weeks before the break-up. Kirsty picked his distress up immediately. Her head bent forward, shielding her face in her pure red curls that somehow seemed to lose their shine.

"Hi," he said, hoping to sound cheery. He didn't.

She bit her lip, as if suppressing a thought. Then she blurted: "Why didn't you just call me?"

"I…" Chang stumbled, "I never thought of it…"

"Why not?" she asked, looking hurt, "Peter said you looked depressed, that I didn't <em>want</em> to come." Her eyes glittered with a watery shine.

"Maybe… Maybe <em>you </em>didn't want me to come?"

No… Chang pleaded in his mind. Please, please don't think that… He brought his hand up and lovingly cupped her cheek, but she jerked her head back as though burnt. Just like Theresa…

"Kirsty…" he begged, "Kirsty, <em>please</em>…" A lonesome tear slid down her face, making Chang's eyes burn. She ruthlessly wiped it off.

"I knew –" Her voice cracked at the 'knew'. "I knew it wouldn't last!" she sobbed. <em>Worse</em> than Theresa. He knew he had to end it here. He knew it would get only worse. Could it? Could it be any worse than this? Yes. She could see the monster he was…

"Kirsty, we can't –"

"I know. I knew all along." She attempted a woeful smile.

"Goodbye, Chang." Time seemed to slow down when Kirsty stepped up the porch, bringing their eyes level. Even more slowly it reached Chang's conscious mind that she closed the gap between them with such simple grace it almost hurt. The brush of their lips took place outside the boundaries of time. So sweet, it was bitter, so brief it took hours to finish, so <em>painful</em> that it felt good… A kiss goodbye…

When they parted it was almost as though it was all just a dream, a very bad dream… They weren't breaking up after all… Both knew full well that they were. Chang's cheeks were wet. Her tears or his, he didn't know. But when Kirsty turned on her heels and ran, he knew that <em>these</em> tears at least were his.

He brought the tips of his fingers to his lips, but he never felt them connect. Would he ever be able to feel anything but the last brush of their lips? He realised he was at the mercy of Pete's comforting embrace, but the other guys standing around him were beyond his understanding.

Kirsty… Would he ever forget her? Would he ever feel love again? …Or had he never experienced love in the first place?
