# Stonebearer

I've been here with him for as long as I can remember. When I was little, I wondered who my parents were, or if they're even alive. Now I guess I've accepted the fact that Guorinn is the only family I have, even if he is but my mentor. Maybe he <em>is</em> related. In a very, <em>very</em> distant way. Of course, I haven't asked him for years. He doesn't like it. Guorinn doesn't care for complications such as family. Guorinn says, that I should be glad I don't have one. According to Guorinn, family tends to claim part of what's rightfully <em>yours</em>. And since it's the Stone that will soon pass into my possession, it's a blessing I've no family-members to take it away.

Since the day I was taken in by Guorinn, perhaps even since the day I was born, I've been trained to one day own the Stone. I won't say it's my destiny, Guorinn doesn't believe in it, but I am in fact one of the very few people with the power to contain it. Guorinn says only one out of a hundred have this strength. One out of a hundred… that can't be more than eighty in the entire country! The other 78 will be the evil warlocks that keep attacking us. They want to steal the Stone and use it for themselves. But Guorinn is well trained in the uses of the Stone. The evil warlocks have Stones, too, but this Stone is the strongest. The ultimate. This is the Stone that defeated the evil king, fifty years ago.

It was Guorinn who defeated the evil king. He was only just appointed as Stonebearer, but already he was incredibly gifted with it. Soon, I'll be appointed Stonebearer. Fifteen years old, and I've already mastered Stonemagic perfectly. Of course, Guorinn won't let me try the actual Stone, but I have lesser Stones to practise with. The thing is, they run out of magic within a month. It's a good thing Guorinn is rich, so we can afford to train me intensively. I think there is a way to recharge them, though. I'm not sure, but Guorinn has never said whether it is or is not possible. I think there is a way, some kind of ritual, probably, but he won't tell me. Yet. As a Stonebearer, I will have to know everything. When I become Stonebearer, I'm sure he will reveal the last of his secrets, and make me a fully-fledged Stonemage.

Maybe the Stone is so powerful because it never runs out. Guorinn has had it for fifty years now, and has performed many great things with it, and there is still magic to spare. Once a year, he lets me try it out, to see if I'm ready. In a way, it's quite depressing. Before I can give it a go, I always feel like my abilities have doubled since the last time I tried. And yet I've never been able to conjure a storm with it. Storm is the hardest of the elemental curses to conjure. You have to move the air in a 10 mile radius, at the least, to frightening speeds in order to get a nice, good storm that will actually snap a tree or two. I can do it with the regular Stones, but I simply can't pull it off with the Stone. But I can try again in 9 weeks and one day. Sixty-four days, then I will prove myself worthy of the title of Stonebearer. Sixty-four, that's eight times eight. Eight squared. Eight miles squared, that's the region where people are afraid to settle nowadays, because mysterious storms, floods, earthquakes and forest fires roam there on a daily bases. That's my doing. Those are my lessons. In nine weeks and a day, I will prove myself ready.

I <em>will</em> become Stonebearer. The youngest Stonebearer ever. And the first ever female Stonemage.

My name is Fiusa. It would do well as to remember it carefully. I am going to do great things in this world. I will save much and defeat many. I might as well be crowned the new queen of this country. Or king, for that matter. Most people can't tell the difference.

It's probably mostly my clothes and my hairdo that are to blame. A dress is terribly uncomfortable in performing Stonemagic, so I spend my days in Guorinn's hand-me-downs. And my hair, well… It kind of paid for my lack of carefulness a few weeks ago. I had gone to the lake to get water, but I had forgotten to look around for intruders. As I bent over the water to fill my bucket, the assassin grabbed me by the hair. I managed to quickly cut myself free with my own dagger, and flooded him using one of de Stones I was carrying. I never found out whether he survived or not. I just ran. Later, Guorinn took care of him. Now, my hair is trimmed to a boyish length, held in a small tail by a piece of string.

So naturally, I look like a boy. A man brings us food every other day. He comes from the nearest village, 11 miles from here. The food is as a thank-you to Guorinn for all the great things he has done. "Mutual protection", this man calls it. Of course, we take his safety into consideration. Two hours surrounding his coming, we cease training, allowing him safe passage. He, too, thinks I look like a boy. He's smart enough to address me as "girl", but he makes comments of my appearance to Guorinn all the time.

I think it's great what he does. Every other day, he travels 11 miles to reach us, constantly aware of possible followers, just to feed the hero and his pupil. And then he goes back those same 11 miles, constantly aware of possible followers, to his village. Sometimes I wonder if he even sleeps at night. I can't ask him, Guorinn won't let me bond with the man. He won't even tell me his name. "He could die at any given moment," he says. "You may never see this man again." I know that. That's why I want to know him so badly.

"That will do, Fiusa," Guorinn called, "It's time."

Obediently, I aborted practice, and ran to Guorinn's side.

"Did you see that?" I panted, "Did you see what that bird did?"

Guorinn nodded, tired, but I could spot the faint smile on his face.

On a subconscious level I spotted the bird flying away top speed, confused and terrified of the strange and un-birdlike behaviour it had shown just moments ago.

I proceeded him to our little cabin, running, arms spread, and laughing like a little child. Guorinn patiently followed.

"Wouldn't it be great to fly?" I asked him later, gulping down my tea.

"Yes, yes," he absently commented.

I got an idea. Trying not to attract attention to myself, I pulled out a Stone, and clutched it tightly.

"Wouldn't it be great to try the Stone <em>today</em>?" I nonchalantly asked him, while trying to concentrate.

Opening his mouth to comment again, Guorinn suddenly snapped back into consciousness.

"<em>No</em>, Fiusa. It would <em>not</em> be great." It was as good as a threat.

I got the threat. Never, <em>ever</em>, try to control me again, the threat said. I obeyed, as usual.

He had a point, of course. It's not like I would enjoy it if someone made me do or say things I didn't want to be doing or saying. If someone controlled my every move, my every thought. I hope I never end up in such a situation.

A little while later, three polite knocks on the door announced the man's arrival.

"Are you alone?" Guorinn asked as usual.

"Yes, sir," the man answered from the other side of the door.

"Have you been followed?"

"No, sir."

"Then enter."

Discreetly the man entered, carrying a large package. He needn't turn around to close the door again. Carefully he placed our ration for the next two days on our modest table.

"Fiusa, store it, will you?" Guorinn asked, again as usual.

"Why don't <em>I</em> store it this time, sir? Surely the girl must dislike it! Besides, it's not like I don't know where it belongs…" the man suggested. We stared at him. This was not as usual.

"Sure," Guorinn finally decided.

Silently, the man began to store our food in the various cabinets of the kitchen. I was still staring at him. My knuckles were going white from squeezing my Stone.

Finally, Guorinn noticed this.

"Fiusa!" he snapped.

Ecstatic, I turned to face my mentor. The man instantly ceased to force the last tealeaves into our slightly undersized tin.

"Did you see that?" I panted, "Did you see what he did!"

Furious, Guorinn cornered me.

"How did you do that?" he hissed.

I was unsure of myself.

"Like I did the bird…! He isn't as strong-willed as you are, Guorinn! He… he's actually quite a push-over!" I stuttered.

"<em>People</em> are not a <em>game</em>!" he snapped.

Guiltily, I looked down.

An uncomfortable silence fell.

"Should I continue, sir?" the man asked.

"Sure," he sighed. And that was <em>not</em> my doing.

I think he wasn't as angry as he looked. After all, I actually controlled a <em>human</em>. I don't think he expected me to master that for at least two weeks.

<strong>o0O0oo0O0oo0O0o</strong>

The next day, things were different. Instead of just having me practice mind-control as scheduled, Guorinn tested me in a battle of wits. I think I need a lot of practice.

All day long, Guorinn had me doing all sorts of chores. Instead of just telling me to, as usual, he forced me to do them on a voluntarily bases. Now I know what the man must have felt like.

"Come on, Fiusa! Show me some resistance!"

I didn't. I chopped wood, swept the floor, prepared lunch and put food on alphabetical order. That night, when I went to sleep, soar all over, I still hadn't eaten all day long.

Five days passed this way. On the sixth day, he showed me the Stone.

"What do you say we test you today, hm?" Guorinn asked me.

Of course I'd say yes. I've been pushing him to let me for months.

"But Guorinn! It's not time yet! I can't try it today!!"

I hope I managed to make it sound ironic. I don't think so, though.

"Of course you can, Fiusa. Here, go on, take it!"

"No, that's alright. I'll wait 'til it's time." I think I actually ground my teeth at that.

Guorinn pocketed the Stone, hesitated, and then went to make tea himself.

By now, I could actually feel what he was making me do. At the moment he was telling me to sit still, be still, and behave like a good girl. Strangely enough, I could also feel my fists clench up as a rage overtook me.

"I do hope you realise I lied," I snapped at him.

Shocked, he spun round, forcing me to look down in shame.

I boldly stared back at him.

Finally, his features softened.

"Perhaps… Perhaps it is time <em>now</em>."

Three polite knocks on the door announced the man's arrival.

I shot Guorinn a surprised look, but I don't know if he saw it.

"Are you alone?" Guorinn asked as usual.

"Yes, sir," the man answered from the other side of the door.

"Have you been followed?"

"No, sir."

"You are early," Guorinn noted.

There was a short pause.

"Yes, sir."

"You are <em>very </em>early."

"Please, sir, can I enter?" the man pleaded.

"Why are you so early?"

"It's my son's birthday today. I want to be home early."

"Give him my regards!" I interfered.

One glare from Guorinn shut me up.

"Fine," he said, "Enter, then."

The man entered.

"Fiusa, store it, will you." said Guorinn.

Grudgingly, I obeyed. I figured that if I kept him satisfied I would still be allowed to try the Stone later.

The man seemed not at ease. Of course, this could partly be blamed to Guorinn, who was glaring at him in a menacing way, but it was still strange.

"Did… did I tell you that it's my son's… birthday… today?" he stuttered.

"Yes," Guorinn said in a low voice, "You did."

"Well, I - I mean my wife and I - we would like to invite you to... well… his birthday."

By now we were both staring at him.

"I mean, he's about the girl's age, and… well… it would do her good to make some friends, I reckon."

"I see." There was no tone in Guorinn's voice. "Fiusa, go fetch some water from the lake."

"But…" I objected.

"Now."

I did attempt to glare at Guorinn, but he never stopped glaring at the man. The door gave quite a bang as I left.

It wasn't fair! I would have become Stonebearer right then and there before the man burst in and ruined everything! It just wasn't <em>fair</em>!!

Any other day, I'd have given anything to meet someone my age, but not <em>today</em>!! <em>It wasn't fair</em>!!

<em>It would do her good to make some friends.</em> What did he mean, it would do me good?! Did he believe I was unhappy here? Did he believe I needed friends? That's total nonsense! Guorinn says friends are like family. I'm <em>lucky</em> I don't have any friends.

Still, the thought puzzled me. Even if I couldn't make a friend, simply having an acquaintance would be fascinating. Someone my age… A <em>boy</em> my age! Had I ever even seen a boy in my entire life?

Well, I must have. I mean, I have a faint idea of what they must look like, maybe a recollection, even. Just like I know what a woman is supposed to look like.

Of course, puberty still frightened me half to death. I knew <em>women</em> had… them, but I never expected to grow them <em>myself</em>. Not that they've grown big, anyway. I still look like a boy. Just one with feminine qualities.

Making my way back to the cabin, I think I spilled at least a third of the water. Not that I cared. It wasn't like Guorinn actually needed it for anything, so it didn't matter anyway.

With little respect for the men's privacy, I kicked the door open. Guorinn stood up just instants after my arrival. The man however remained seated.

"Have you decided yet?" I practically barked at Guorinn.

"No," he said uncomfortably, "Fiusa, do <em>you</em> want to go?"

<em>Take every opportunity that presents itself</em>.

"Can we still try when we get back?" I asked.

"Hopefully, yes." He seemed to doubt it, but I didn't care. I would <em>make</em> him keep his promise.

Slowly, I nodded.

The man sighed.

"Let's go, then…"

As we walked to where the man had hid his horse, I couldn't help but notice we weren't taking Tluda, our ancient mule, to aid us in our eleven-mile travel.

"Guorinn?" I therefor asked, "Why did we leave the donkey behind?"

He ignored me with all his might.

"I doubt you'll need it," the man bitterly answered instead.

I didn't know what to think of it. On one hand an uptight Guorinn, eyes flashing at the bushes surrounding us like he expected them to jump up and walk, and on the other hand the swearing man, fiddling the rope tying his horse to a branch like he had never seen a knot before.

And why was the man using just his left hand? No wonder he couldn't untie his horse, he was stubbornly keeping one hand in his pocket! What could possibly be in his pocket that he couldn't let go?

Miraculously, the man managed to disconnect the branch from the rope.

"Sir," he said in a forced tone, "It's going to be a long journey. You shouldn't have to walk all the way. Ride instead."

Guorinn visibly swallowed, yet silently agreed. He climbed it with very little grace.

Maybe he was just afraid of horses. Guorinn the great, his dreams tormented with the idea of riding like the wind. The idea sounded almost too funny to be true, but when I saw how his knuckles turned white from grasping the reigns, it didn't seem that illogical. I mentally chuckled.

Of course, he didn't <em>really</em> ride the horse. The man led it across the well-concealed path, still not taking his right hand from his pocket.

Gradually I slipped into blankly staring ahead. The whole situation was starting to freak me out. If even <em>Guorinn</em> was afraid, I could only begin to imagine the immense fear I should have felt.

We walked on like this for miles, Guorinn clutching the reigns, the man pocketing his hand, and me, Fiusa, trying to make sense of it all.

Both men seemed very on guard. Why was that? Was it really because of the horse? Were they simply worried they'd be followed?

About a month ago I lost my hair fighting off an assassin. They hadn't been able to find us in ages now. Were we really in danger? The horse seemed nervous as well now. Was that because of us? Or was there more to it?

The man stopped, pulling over the horse with him.

"Child," Guorinn said, "Hide."

"There's no pride in hiding!" I countered, a bit touchy. All the nerves were getting to me.

"But those who stay will die!" shouted the man.

The horse whinnied.

"FIRE!" shouted a raw voice.

Instantly five arrows zoomed right at us.

On instinct I clutched my Stone to evade the arrow.

The man, too, was scarcely missed.

Guorinn was staked simultaneously by the last three arrows.

Horribly slow he tumbled off the horse.

"GUORINN!" I screamed, running towards him.

"He's a Mage!" someone yelled.

I felt several evil warlocks trying to stop me.

I set fire to one of them.

"GUORINN!" I screamed again.

My voice sounded strange, so unlike my own.

"Don't let him near! He might heal him!"

More wills battled mine.

I could no longer will my legs to move.

"Guorinn!" I sobbed.

Someone grabbed me from behind.

I managed to stab him with my dagger.

Horrible pains spread over me instantly.

Did I scream? I might've.

"Don't hurt him!" the man screamed desperately, "Please, don't hurt him!"

The pain stopped, but the very instant many hands grabbed me, held me down.

I tried to struggle, but I could only will my muscles to do so much. It wasn't enough.

My Stone was taken from me.

"Guorinn…" I sobbed again.

"Guorinn is dead," a man said with certain pride.

The evil warlocks sighed in relief.

And that was it.

My life has never existed of anything more than Guorinn, the man, and the Stone.

The Stone is taken from us.

The man has betrayed us.

Guorinn left us.

Guorinn…

Who am I, Fiusa, without Guorinn there to guide me?

Where am I to go, if not to follow Guorinn?

I might as well die right here.

But I'm already gone…

"Milord!" the man called to whom he supposed their leader, "You will not find the Stone in his pocket."

The soldier that was just searching out his coat, ceased.

"I have it right here," the man continued.

From his pocket he took the Stone, still clutched in his right hand.

"You…!" I hissed furiously, "<em>Murderer</em>!"

More hands and wills restrained me.

The man smiled at me strangely.

"Silly child," he spoke, looking at me in a way I've never seen him look, "You mourn nothing but your teacher, who had nothing left to teach…"

One or two warlocks had the nerve to laugh.

I didn't respond. I <em>couldn't</em> respond. For one, the man's strange words astounded me. Guorinn said things like that all the time, but the man had always been simple. Secondly, with the Stone still clutched in his hand, he forced me not to twitch in the slightest. Not a single detail changed in my furious exterior, but I was no longer furious. I was confused. The man was not a Stonemage. He could not control Stones, let alone <em>the </em>Stone. The man knew nothing of my progress using Stones. <em>The man was not Guorinn</em>.

Right?

I tore my eyes away from him and stared at the body that used to house Guorinn's soul. It wasn't there. Even without my Stones, I could tell. But had it really moved on to the man's body, rather than to the other realm?

<em>They switched back home</em>.

It had to be! They switched while I'd been fetching some water! It's the only reasonable explanation.

I know, in theory, the works of soul switching, but Guorinn never let me try. The risks are too big and the stakes to high. Gambling with your life, he called it. He gambled with many things, but never with our own lives.

Cautious of fear that I'd hurt him, the leader of the assassins stepped up and took the Stone from the man. Obviously he himself was not a Stonemage.

Pocketing the Stone, he climbed his own horse again. From the saddle he retrieved a small pouch, and threw it at the man, at Guorinn. It chinked as it hit the earth.

My mind was clear now. I could tell that only seven of the 15 people really were Stonemages. Besides the leader, the other seven were mere soldiers. I could see from the telltale swords carried by the soldiers.

One of the soldiers coughed.

"What?" the leader barked at him.

"What about the boy? He'll want revenge. He will… He <em>might</em> come after us."

Some other soldiers shivered, and I felt a strange surge of pride. They were <em>afraid</em> of me.

"Milord!" the man, Guorinn, spoke up.

"Yes…" the leader sighed, wanting to go home already.

"Let me take him in. I have a son his age; he'll fit right in. I will teach him the error of his master's ways and give him a life without those wretched Stones. Of course, I'll see to it that he'll never touch one of those ever again in his entire life. That way you needn't have a child's blood on your hands."

Some of the Stonemages seemed cynical about this, but their leader ignored them.

"Fine," he said, and turned his horse around.

His soldiers and Stonemages also climbed their horses. One of them collected Guorinn's corpse and draped it over his saddle in front of him. I think I may have sobbed. Did I sob?

The leader omitted a raw battle cry, and all fifteen horses took off.

With trouble the man Guorinn restrained the man's horse from following.

"Guorinn," I said.

"Yes?" the man responded.

"What did you mean, 'I mourn nothing but my teacher, who has nothing left to teach'?"

The man Guorinn smiled.

"You couldn't figure it out yet?" he said, his eyes shining with joy, "You would have passed the test, Fiusa. No doubt in my mind."

"But…" I stumbled, "You gave away the Stone…"

"To save my life and yours, yes. It doesn't matter, yet. The Stone is useless in their hands."

"They've no one trained for its uses?"

The man Guorinn hesitated.

"They probably have. But it will do them no good. The Stone is for our hands only."

I nodded solemnly. I didn't quite understand, but it made sense, after all. Perhaps the Stone wielded only to those with good intentions.

"Listen, Fiusa, I have to leave you here. Soon the warlocks will find out I'm alive, and look for me in this body. I can't have you with me, or it'll ease their search."

"But I <em>want </em>to go with you…"

"No. You will go to the village as planned. Take the entrance furthest from here."

"Will I be a boy or a girl?" I asked him, reluctantly accepting the plan.

"Boy," he said after a moment's doubt, "You lost your parents in the war. No one will ever question that."

We were both silent for a while.

"What about you?" I finally asked.

He sighed.

"I will evade them for as long as I can. The Stone is useless without me. Fiusa, I want you to search for the Stone, to avenge yourself upon my killers! The evil warlocks will try to bring the evil king's grandson to the throne, but it must never happen! When you find it, conjure a storm. I will be watching, and give you strength."

My eyes were stinging. I'd just found out I hadn't lost my life's companion after all, but now he was leaving me all the same. It just wasn't fair!

He grabbed my shoulders and looked me in the eyes intensely.

"Listen to me, and listen carefully!" he spoke urgently, "The people of this country have been brainwashed. They think we're evil and the warlocks are good. Don't let their foul words provoke you! And above all, you <em>must not</em> give your opinion on the matter! They'll think you a traitor of the one that is actually the traitor himself, and they'll try to harm you. Agree with them if you must, Fiusa! But in your mind, always know the truth. We'll free them from the tyranny. In time they'll understand."

I nodded.

His conscience eased, Guorinn let me go, and climbed the horse that used to belong to the village man.

"Guorinn?"

"Yes, child?" he asked, facing me for what could be the last time.

"What will my name be?"

Guorinn thought it over.

"Gwenron," he suggested finally.

It sounded natural. Of course, it would take a while for me to get used to it, but it was a name I could live with.

"Farewell, Gwenron!" he spoke, and he rode off.

Suddenly something hit me.

"Guorinn!" I called after him.

Yards away, he stopped the horse again to face me.

"How does one recharge their Stones?" I finally had the courage to ask.

"By dying!" he shouted back.

One last spurt of his horse, and he was gone.

For good.
