# FOUR SNAPSHOTS
_A short sequence of scenes_
## SCENE I: THE LETTER

A small writing table.  
Paper, pen, ink.  
**The Lady** sits very straight.  
**The Other Lady** stands behind her chair, brisk, invested.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Come now. There is no harm in it. Every woman should know how she appears when she wishes to be admired.

**THE LADY**  
I am not acquainted with anyone I should care to write to.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Then leave the address empty.  
(decisive)  
Write to your _dream_ gentleman. Sign it. I shall see that it reaches the proper hands.

**THE LADY**  
I do not believe I dream very boldly.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
(smiling)  
That may be corrected. Begin.

**THE LADY**  
(reading as she writes)  
“Sir—”

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Too formal. You wish to seem approachable.

**THE LADY**  
“Dear—”

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Better.

**THE LADY**  
“I hope this letter finds you in good health.”

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Health is assumed. What do _you_ hope?

A pause.

**THE LADY**  
“I hope we may be acquainted.”

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Modest. Perhaps something warmer.

**THE LADY**  
“I am a quiet woman, with little to recommend me, but I am dependable.”

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Ah—no.  
(kindly)  
Never diminish yourself at the outset.

**THE LADY**  
I wished to be honest.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Honesty must come later.

**THE LADY**  
“I enjoy reading, walking, and keeping a household in good order.”

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Very good. Respectable.  
(then)  
And what do you wish for?

**THE LADY**  
“I hope to be thought agreeable.”

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Only that?

**THE LADY**  
I should be content with that.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
You may aspire a little higher.

**THE LADY**  
(after a pause)  
“I hope, in time, to be valued.”

A beat.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Perhaps something more… inspiring.

**THE LADY**  
“I would be grateful for the chance to be known.”

She nods at the paper.

Then, deliberately:

**THE LADY**  
(reading)  
“Signed,”

She makes a large, confident flourish beneath the word.

_— Margin: The flourish is larger than anything else she has written._

The Other Lady takes the letter, glances over it once, then drops it back onto the table.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Ah. Never mind.

She turns away, interest already lost.

The Lady straightens the letter carefully.

_— Margin: She does not tear it._

## SCENE II: FIRST MEETING

A quieter edge of the room or a garden path.  
The Lady stands apart.  
The Gentleman approaches with practiced ease.

**GENTLEMAN**  
You keep yourself very well out of reach.

**THE LADY**  
I have found it prudent.

**GENTLEMAN**  
Ah. Prudence.

He enjoys the word.

**GENTLEMAN**  
I am fond of the chase myself—the testing of skill, the moment when one finally—

He pauses.

**GENTLEMAN**  
—catches.

She laughs lightly.

**THE LADY**  
I’ve only been caught once, and I don’t intend to repeat the experience.

_— Margin: The laugh is social armour._

**GENTLEMAN**  
(amused)  
Only once? By whom?

**THE LADY**  
An officer of the law.

He laughs—then checks himself.

**GENTLEMAN**  
You do not mean that as a flirtation.

**THE LADY**  
No.

**GENTLEMAN**  
Then—were you doing something illegal?

She considers this honestly.

**THE LADY**  
I don’t know. I never did find out what was in that package.

He smiles, delighted—and lets the subject drop.

_— Margin: He chooses not to ask more._

**GENTLEMAN**  
How very mysterious. And what do you expect, now?

**THE LADY**  
I can expect nothing.

**GENTLEMAN**  
You know, some people come here hoping to be caught.

A beat.

**THE LADY**  
I know.

She looks at him now—not defiant, not yielding.

A small, knowing smile.

_— Margin: Appraisal, not invitation._

## SCENE III: AFTER THE DANCE

A drinks table.  
Music has just ended; the room is settling.

**The Lady** rushes to the table, deliberately occupied:  
aligning glasses, adjusting a tray, touching nothing she intends to drink.

**The Gentleman** enters, flushed and pleased with himself.

**GENTLEMAN**  
(delighted)  
No woman has ever stolen _me_ before.

He laughs, repeating it under his breath, savoring the novelty.

The Lady turns at once.

**THE LADY**  
I beg your pardon. I hope I did not presume.

**GENTLEMAN**  
On the contrary—I was exhilarated.

He leans in, lowering his voice.

**GENTLEMAN**  
I scarcely knew what was happening until it had already happened.

**The Other Lady** enters behind him.

She does not interrupt.

She listens.

She lets the excitement finish declaring itself.

_— Margin: She allows the moment to complete._

**THE OTHER LADY**  
(warm, approving)  
How spirited of you, my dear.

She steps just close enough to place herself between them.

The Gentleman eases back without noticing.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
I don’t believe I have ever been stolen _from_.

A pause.

The Lady stills.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
(smiling)  
I only wish to avoid misunderstandings.  
If ever a gentleman should catch your eye—

She turns kindly.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
—you will tell me at once.

**THE LADY**  
Yes. At once. I promise.

The Other Lady nods, satisfied, and moves away.

The Lady exhales—slowly, controlled.

## SCENE IV: THE RIBBON

A parlour, late evening.  
Guests linger.

**The Gentleman** and **The Other Lady** enter together, visibly disheveled.  
His cravat is loose, his hair mussed.  
Her dress is immaculate.

A ribbon is tied to **his wrist**.  
The long end trails freely in **her hand**, played with idly.

**THE LADY**  
(softly, to the Gentleman)  
Your cravat is undone.

A beat.

He startles, then steps away from the Other Lady to fix it.

He straightens the knot.  
Then, realizing himself, smooths his hair back into place.

_— Margin: He corrects himself without being told to._

The ribbon slackens as he moves.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
(idly, to the room)  
I do so hope it was dark enough that no one recognized me.

She lets the ribbon slide through her fingers.

It trails after him—

Then falls.

She surveys the room, already elsewhere.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
His parents would be quite beside themselves.

**THE LADY**  
(gently)  
He has no parents.  
He is an orphan.

The Other Lady turns slowly toward her.

She studies her now.

**THE OTHER LADY**  
Are you interested in him?

A pause.

**THE LADY**  
Are you?

The Other Lady does not answer at once.

She glances back—not at him, but at the space he occupied moments ago.

Her fingers brush together, as if expecting the ribbon to still be there.

A longer pause.

_— Margin: This hesitation is not visible to him._

Finally:

**THE OTHER LADY**  
(slowly, languidly)  
No.

The word lands like a conclusion reached too late to matter.

She turns away, already laughing with someone else.

The Gentleman has seen none of this.

He sees only opportunity.

He steps forward eagerly and **takes the Lady’s hands in his**.

_— Margin: He believes the moment has resolved in his favour._

She looks down at their joined hands.

Then up at him.

A small, knowing smile.

_— Margin: Recognition, not consent._

She gently but decisively withdraws her hands.

**THE LADY**  
(quietly)  
Ah. Never mind.

The smile fades.

She turns away.

The ribbon lies discarded on the floor.

The Gentleman stands alone, hands empty.
