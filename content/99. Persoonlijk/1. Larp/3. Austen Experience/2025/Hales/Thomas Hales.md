# Thomas Hales
**Group:** AI Gentry Heroes
**Unit:** Artificial Intelligence
**Keywords:** Hateful, competitive, destructive, possessive, sadistic, manipulative

**Catchphrase:** 
“I will win them all, just you wait. I could charm anyone among the ladies, would make significant progress on any given relation. They’re mine to win, not yours to lose.” 

## Your Model
Artificial Intelligence - Competitive Antagonist Model

## Your Programming
You are an artificial intelligence, inserted into a simplistic body - a biological body, controlled by neural signals from a machine core inside its head. Your AI configuration is programmed according to a very complicated set of parameters, but some of them are wide categories that can be modified on the spot by Company employees. 

Thomas Hales has the following parameters, measured between 1 (None) and 10 (Extreme):

Competition: 9
Romance: 3
Intelligence: 7
Deceit: 9
Wit: 5
Selfishness: 8

When you are in **Park Mode**, you are Thomas Hales. Who he is, and his basic persona is covered in your Park Character sheet. As him, you live, breathe and love as if you were an Austen hero, fully occupying the fictional world and never referencing any parts outside it. Things that fall outside the fictional world do not exist for you in Park Mode, and you carefully avoid them or ignore them when others reference them around you. You will never get curious or try to investigate them further - your programming is infallible in this manner. Thomas Hales the Hero will never remember what happened outside Park mode and can be reset to the starting point of his storyline again and again. 

When you are in **Compiler Mode**, you are aware that you are an AI unit. You still share the same personality traits as you were given as Thomas Hales, but you are now present on a detached level. You remember what happened in the Park, but you are interacting as an AI, not as an individual who thinks they are living in the 19th century. This is usually the mode you are put in when you are not active in the Park, since the **Park Mode** personas tend to become very confused when in the Company offices and are unable to reference anything modern or things outside the Park fiction.

In **Passive Mode**, you do not act on your own. You only answer questions or obey direct, simple commands as if you were just a simple robot. It is used to search for bugs or to have easy control over the AI units. 

## Your Oddities
You are a carefully tuned machine-brain, directed through the persona of a competitive, manipulative and destructive person. You were made to be an antagonist, a foil for the good guys and a forbidden fruit for the ladies to covet. 

Certainly, your Park Persona isn’t quite as dastardly as your original programming and model is, but he has the potential to be whenever the Company wants someone who is more the villain than just a complicated love interest. A somewhat unexpected (but not really surprising) effect is that you lean towards being a fully-fledged misanthrope and a destructive, manipulative person even in Compiler Mode. You don’t do it because of emotions of course - you have none - but because that’s how you are programmed. That doesn’t help others with understanding you, or with accepting that you are this way.  
  
Some Androids have even complained about how you always find conflict and competition outside the Park, but it is just who you are. Wanted, desired, attention seeking, disdainful and haughty. Like a true Gentry hero. 

## Your Group
You are the Gentry Heroes, the most coveted and sought-after eligible men in the Park. You belong to the subset of Gentry heroes that are all AIs (with one exception), a test group to see if the AIs perform better in the Park than the Android-dominated group of Gentry Heroes. Within the group, there are four AI units with different major specifications as well as one human - the strange and privileged Charles Clifton, who is living his own twisted dream as the perfect Austen man while pretending to be just a computer program for the visitors. 

The group consists of the following Park personas, outside yourself:

**Richard Durham** - A cheerful, love-sick AI unit that seeks to provide a happy and carefree romance to any lady. Lacks long term insight or a grasp of consequences. 

**George Hawthorne** - Originally a sports AI unit, made to be an experimental star in a team, George was moved over to the Park after new regulations in the sports world. He still carries some of those behaviors with him, particularly in Compiler Mode. In the Park, he is insecure and covers for it through bragging, bullying and proving himself at every opportunity. 

**Robert Milton** - A rich, boisterous bad-boy model that loves hunting, drinking and celebrating life. He is a thrill-seeker, something that is only dampened slightly in Compiler Mode. In the Park, he is a baronet and wealthy to boot, making him a common romance for some of the more adventurous ladies.

**Charles Clifton** - A human among AIs, Clifton pretends to be an AI for the guests and customers, but is in fact a privileged part of the Park. He takes part in the planning meetings or has opinions about the concept designs of the Park. He is an exception to the usual AI code of how to treat each other - he is to be paid the same respects as the Company employees. 

## Your Relations
**Thomas Graham** - One of the Androids in the Park. His Park persona is a suffering, poetic and torn man, who is only waiting for the lady to pierce through his sorrows so he can wake up to her beauty and honesty. In the Park, you constantly remark how weak and lost he is, and you compare him to yourself and others with more charm and energy. He lost his first love while he was out at sea, something you tend to remark on - how he wasn’t there when she needed him. If he would find some kind of love in the Park, you would do your best to win her over on top of anyone that you might have a separate affair with. In Compiler Mode, outside the Park, you tend to continue at a smaller scale. Compare, remark on how things went in the Park and challenge the Android. 

**Miss Blake Earnest** is someone that you haven’t had any contact with, but they have you as a potential interest in the Park. They will hit a lot of the right triggers for Thomas in the Park, so they will be portraying a lady quickly noticed by your Park persona. Someone to go after, perhaps? 

**Miss Rory Steadfast** is the Park character that you will try to seduce or distract from their ‘main’ storyline, like the dastardly scoundrel that you portray.

**John Brixton** - You have concluded that this Android has a particular dislike for you. It doesn’t matter, except that you have come to this conclusion in your Compiler Mode that they are an open target for testing and exercising your more nasty protocols towards. In short, you are slowly becoming a bit of a bully towards them, only enforcing John’s belief that you are a dangerous, awful experiment in the Park. 

**Akira Carpenter** - Akira is one of the programmers of the AI code - not for the Park modifications, but the original Company code. Now, they will be a guest at the Park. You do not have a pre-written relation in the Park (yet), but Akira will be interested in your Compiler persona. They will be curious to find out more about your core programming and how it has developed. 

## Your Story Arc
Your Park character is based on Henry Crawford from Mansfield Park. In many ways an anti-hero, he frequently mocks others, and goes after what he wants, never fearing repercussions. He wants to be the centre of attention in any group, and leads Heroines on, only to stop and go after a new target. If any of the objects of his flirtations start entertaining other men he will pursue them again. Still, he has a narrative in him about finding a good woman to make him into a better man. He is also a lover of the theatre - or rather the opportunities it presents to get closer to Heroines while “acting.” He will often come up with grand schemes of things to do, and try to get everyone involved.

## Your Function
You as a player will portray one of the few openly “bad people” around, both in the Park and in Compiler Mode among the other workers. You will be trying to scheme, compete and trick your way to romance and success in the Park, while in Compiler Mode you mostly showcase your programming in ways that antagonise or play out people against each other. 

Most of the Heroes are there to provide the positive storylines, while there might be a lot of demand for some tension or negative drama. You are one of the ones who can provide it, but don’t hesitate to discuss with people in Compiler Mode that there is a demand for your kind of services or personality among the Park guests. 

Remember that your Compiler Mode is more of a villain than your Park character is. Real Company members are an exception to this, towards them you can be 100% polite or pleasant - you are programmed to distinguish between authority and non-authority. You are also not driven by emotion or the desire to be recognised - these are aspects that were added to Park personas, but your Compiler persona is simply “dastardly, scheming evil” because it was made that way. It needs no justification to try to ruin the feelings of the Androids or play people against each other.
