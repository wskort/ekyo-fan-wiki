### Narrator/[[Verteller]]
Het perspectief van waaruit het verhaal wordt verteld/getoond/ervaren. Is dit slechts 1 personage, wisselt het tussen verschillende personages, of wordt het van buiten de verhaalwereld bekeken? Binnen larp zou dit respectievelijk spelers, figuranten en spelleiders kunnen omschrijven. 
    
#### Polyphony
In literature, polyphony (Russian: полифония) is a feature of narrative, which includes a diversity of simultaneous points of view and voices. Caryl Emerson describes it as "a decentered authorial stance that grants validity to all voices.” The concept was introduced by Mikhail Bakhtin, using a metaphor based on the musical term polyphony.
    
### [[Paratekst]] 
De omliggende informatie (geschreven tekst, beeldmateriaal, etc.) die niet deel is van de verhaalwereld zelf, maar wel een kader geeft aan de tekst zelf. In larp context is dit bijvoorbeeld de settinginformatie en de verwachtingsmanagement. 
    

## [[Ludologie]]
Marie-Laure Ryan (2001, 2005, 2006) is concerned with a more general application of narratology to IDN. She reminds us that both Barthes and Bremond originally conceived narratology as transcending discipline and media (Ryan, 2006, p. 4) and points out how contemporary narratology sees narrative as a media-independent, basic cognitive construct (see Herman, 2000, 2002). (...)  

In Ryan’s view, the ludologists take Prince’s original (1987) (and later modified, see Prince, 2003) definition of narrative as the central tenet of narratology since it can be denounced as not applicable to computer games for seemingly excluding mimetic forms of narrative. Ryan’s solution to this proclaimed conflict is an extension of Roger Callois’ (1961) distinction between two kinds of play—paidia and ludus. Ryan takes paidia to describe playing games of make-believe (Ryan, 2005) that require participants to play a role and thus actively use their imagination. In contrast, ludus denotes engagement with games that are played in a competitive spirit (Ryan, 2005), for example sports games. This means that computer games that invite make believe activity—like The Sims (Wright, 2000)—can be described as narratives regardless of strong ludic elements.  

Ryan’s application of narratology results in a rich methodological toolkit for the analysis of IDN. Her crucial introduction of paidia as a dimension of computer games offers a perspective that reconciles narrative and games. However, Ryan preserves a dichotomy on the core level that depicts interactivity as a distraction to narrative (“interactivity is not a feature that facilitates the construction of narrative meaning” \[Ryan 2006, p. 117\]). This view seems premature, given the early stage of IDN as an expressive form. Interactivity might very well be able to evoke the cognitive structures Ryan takes to be the core meaning of narrative.

### [[Transformatie]] (van het geheel)
Janet Murray (1997) defínes the phenomenological categories of agency, immersion, and transformation. She sees agency as the experience of affecting a meaningful and intelligible change in the digital artefact, while immersion describes the ability of a digital artefact to hold our interest. The resulting experience, Murray notes, would be transformative in the sense of a kaleidoscope, which recombines its elements into new forms with every turn. Murray’s definitions provide a well-defined framework for understanding digital media artefacts.

## Interactive narrative
Henry Jenkins (2004) insists that narrative in interactive media will not work in the same way that narrative in other media functions, and we should therefore expect different properties. This important insight is the starting point from which he considers four possible modes for the integration of interactivity and narrative: evocative, enacted, embedded and emergent. The evocative mode describes narratives that reference prior stories in other media. An example would be a Star Wars Game that refers to the movie series. Enacted narratives allow the user to act out specific roles within an existing narrative universe, for example the career of a Star Wars star fleet fighter pilot. Embedded narratives convey information by means of spatially distributed narrative-infused encounters, as exemplified in the game Myst. Finally, emergent narratives appear in unstructured but rule based game worlds like The Sims that provide players with the tools to construct stories of their own. 
### [[Evocative]]
Reference prior stories from other media

### [[Enacted]]
Act out specific roles in a narrative universe

### [[Embedded]]
Convey information through spatially distributed narrative-infused encounters (see also: environmental storytelling) 

### [[Emergent]]
Can appear naturally when players have the tools to construct stories of their own 

## IDN theories (Interactive Digital Narrative)  
	(Koenitz et al. Interactive Digital Narrative, Routledge, NY 2015)
    
### [[Protostory]]
Denotes the concrete content of an IDN work as a space of potential narratives. Any realised narrative experience is related to the respective protostory through a process of instantiation. 
    
### [[Narrative design]]
The structure within a protostory that describes a flexible presentation of the narrative. This includes the segmentation and sequencing of elements as well as the connections between them. 
  
 ### [[Narrative vectors]]
 Substructures within a narrative design that provide a specific direction. Do not work as isolated structures, but rather in connection with the preceding and following parts of the narrative. Functionally equivalent to plot points in static media.
